## What It Will Look Like

A tall Mouth Tipoff boarding tower stands at the front-left. A violet Esophagus Drive chute slopes toward the raised, open Stomach Spin Zone funnel at the back-left. The marble enters near the funnel rim, curves toward its outlet, and drops into a guided collector. Three descending track runs with two broad U-turns form the Small-Intestine Crossover. The Large-Intestine Home Stretch descends to a rectum holding cup beside an anus-themed exit arch at The Final Buzzer. Violet, black, white, and silver signs, court-line queue rails, a scoreboard, and basketball scenery connect the digestive route to the Valkyries theme. Basketball decorations never obstruct the marble's path.

**Main route:** Mouth -> esophagus -> stomach funnel -> small-intestine turns -> large-intestine curve -> rectum / anus exit.

**Science distinction:** Gravity drives this model; real food moves through muscular action, including peristalsis. The marble remains intact, but real food is broken down and nutrients are absorbed. Heights, slopes, and organ proportions are ride-design choices, not anatomical measurements.

The main straight intestine runs descend 2 cm over 20 cm, a 10% grade. The turns also descend; never make a flat U-turn simply because both ends share a plan-view row. Draw intermediate height marks around each bend.

The funnel's center outlet drops vertically into its collector; that is the only intended short drop. Enclose this transition with a smooth guide so the marble cannot fall off the display. Adjust the collector's height to the funnel you actually use.

These points are centerline planning locations, not exact cutting templates. Foam thickness, bend radius, and funnel depth require small adjustments. Keep all track edges at least 2 cm inside the base and fit the dry layout before fixing supports.

## Build It In This Order

### 1. Base And Tower

Glue cardboard layers together with alternating corrugation direction. Make the 40 cm start tower from a broad folded box column, roughly 10-12 cm square, rather than a single thin paper tube. Add triangular braces on at least two sides and broad glued feet. Keep decoration lightweight. Check that the tower stays vertical when the marble is loaded.

### 2. Prove The Track Works First

Before building the whole ride, test a 20 cm foam channel with a 2 cm fall using the actual marble. Release without a push. If it stalls, check rubbing, dents, and tape lips before increasing the fall. Then test one broad U-turn separately. If a tight bend pinches the foam, widen it or make the turn from short, smoothly joined sections on a cardboard cradle.

### 3. Mouth Release And Esophagus

Build an open mouth arch around the start, with teeth that do not touch the marble. Make a tongue-colored track floor. A small removable cardboard gate holds the marble at the 40 cm mark; lifting the gate starts the ride. Support the esophagus chute at the top, midpoint, and funnel entry. Use shallow side walls or a deep enough channel to retain the marble.

### 4. Stomach Funnel And Collector

Mount the funnel in a snug hole in a cardboard platform, supported by three or four braced legs. Do not rely on glue at one point. Add a stomach-shaped cutaway wall around its outside, leaving the bowl visible. Guide the chute into the bowl close to its edge and in the direction of the rim, so the marble may circle before reaching the center. The number of circles is not guaranteed.

Under the outlet, make a wide-mouth collector with high sides and a smooth curved floor leading into the next foam track. The funnel stem should project into the collector's guarded opening without blocking it. Avoid an exposed gap. Test this assembly repeatedly before adding any stomach decoration.

**Simpler stomach substitute:** Replace the funnel and collector with a single broad, open C-shaped foam turn mounted on a descending cardboard platform. Connect the esophagus directly to it, then to the first intestine run. Spread the same 28 cm-to-19 cm fall along the curved approach. Keep the stomach-shaped cutaway scenery. This is easier to tune and avoids funnel jams.

### 5. Small-Intestine Switchbacks

Build three approximately 20 cm straight runs with two broad U-turns. Use the height table, placing adjustable folded-card cradles under joints, each turn, and roughly every 10-15 cm of unsupported foam. Lower the floor continuously along the route. Tape joints smooth with the downstream channel slightly lower, never protruding upward. Bank the outside of turns slightly if needed, but test rather than assuming it will prevent escapes.

Keep paper villi and nutrient arrows on the outside of the rolling channel. Add a separate magnified villus drawing with a capillary and a lacteal. Do not put paper fingers in the marble's path.

### 6. Outer Curve And Finish

Continue into a purple-decorated large-intestine curve at the lower right. Keep the actual channel wide enough for the marble; make the organ look wider with scenery, not a sudden uncontrolled widening of the rolling floor. Guide the final track into the lined catch cup. The cup represents the rectum holding station. A clearly labeled anus exit arch beside the cup shows the final opening; remove the marble by hand after the run.

### 7. Make It Look Like A Park

Add the **VALKYRIES ADVENTURE PARK** sign at the front and **THE VALKYRIE FAST BREAK** sign above Mouth Tipoff. Use a scoreboard-style ride sign, court-line queue rails, a ticket booth, Fuel the Fast Break Cafe, courtside benches, bins, and a visitor walkway. A decorative hoop can stand beside the exit; the marble must stop in its cup, not jump through the hoop. Put liver, gallbladder, and pancreas scenery near the stomach but clear of the track. Use dashed paper arrows for secretions entering the small intestine; no marble branch enters those organs.

Type and print the organ labels. Number the route 1-6 to match the sketch. Leave all rolling sections visible for demonstration and troubleshooting. Any roof must be partly cut away.

### 8. Tune Before Final Gluing

Test each section and then the whole route. Use temporary tape and removable cardboard shims until the route works. Aim for **10 consecutive complete runs from the same release point without a push** before decorating or claiming reliable operation. This is a target, not a result already achieved. Glue the supports only after testing, allow them to dry, and retest after decoration and transportation.

| Problem | First fix |
| --- | --- |
| Marble stops at a join | Remove the uphill tape lip; align and smooth the floors. |
| Marble slows on a straight | Remove a sag or rubbing point; increase the local fall slightly if needed. |
| Marble leaves a curve | Widen and smooth the curve, raise the outer wall, or reduce the preceding drop. |
| Marble jams in funnel | Measure the entire outlet bore; use a larger opening or the C-turn substitute. |
| Marble escapes below funnel | Enclose the collector transition; align the outlet above its receiving area. |
| Marble rebounds from finish | Deepen or widen the catch cup, soften its liner, and reduce the last drop. |
| Tower wobbles | Widen its feet and add cross-braces before continuing. |

**Optional science investigation:** After the ride works, compare 38, 40, and 42 cm release heights on the first chute, changing only that starting section and keeping the downstream route fixed. Record five run times and completion counts at each height. Use slow-motion video if available. Do not assume greater height always gives a better run: more speed may cause escapes.

## Safety And Science Sources

Use only one marble at a time. Store it in a closed container away from young children; marbles are a choking hazard. Never launch marbles toward people. Adults handle knives, foam cutting, funnel modifications, and hot glue. Keep the base on a level table, do not lean on the tower, and never put actual food in the model.
